Apple M1 to M6 benchmarks Geekbench 7, Geekbench 6 and Cinebench on one chart

Each row is a generation, each dot is a chip: base, Pro, Max and Ultra. Switch between Geekbench 7 (CPU and Metal GPU), Geekbench 6, Cinebench 2026 and Cinebench 2024. As of November 2026 only the base M6 has been released.

Key takeaways

  • +83%single-core, M6 vs M1

    The base M6 scores 4,004 in Geekbench 7 single-core vs 2,191 for the M1, and more than any M5 chip (best: M5 Max, 3,733). In Cinebench 2026 single-core it's 824 vs 419, almost exactly 2×.

  • 2.6×multi-core, M6 vs M1

    The base M6 reaches 21,868 in Geekbench 7 multi-core. It beats every M1 and M2 Pro and Max and the M3 Pro, but not the M3 Max, the M4 Pro or any Ultra.

  • 51,848M5 Ultra, GB7 multi-core

    The M5 Ultra is the fastest multi-core and GPU chip: 343,652 in Metal and 16,971 in Cinebench 2026 multi-core, 32%, 39% and 40% above the M3 Ultra. There is no M4 Ultra, so the Ultra line goes from M3 straight to M5.

  • ~7%single-core spread within a generation

    All tiers of one generation use the same CPU core design, and Pro, Max and Ultra parts often clock slightly higher. The real differences between them are in multi-core and GPU.

Sources and method

Geekbench 7: Geekbench Browser, Mac Benchmarks, average of submitted results (at least 5 per Mac). For each test the highest-scoring Mac model in the chart is used, so a chip's three Geekbench numbers can come from different Macs. Geekbench 7 uses a different scale than Geekbench 6, so don't compare these numbers with older GB6 charts.

Geekbench 6: Geekbench Browser now lists only Geekbench 7 results, so Geekbench 6 CPU scores come from notebookcheck.net (averages of the devices they tested, best configuration per test). The M3 Ultra is from cpu-monkey.com. There is no reliable Geekbench 6 result for the M1 Ultra, and no Geekbench 6 Metal data.

Cinebench 2026: cpu-monkey.com. Multi-core for M2 Max, M2 Ultra and M3 Pro and single-core for M2 Max, M3 Pro, M5 Pro and M5 Max come from notebookcheck.net. The M1 Ultra has no measured multi-core result; its value is an estimate from Drop & Render (hollow dot). Drop & Render's estimates for other Apple chips mostly come in below measured scores, so the M1 Ultra value is probably on the low side. There's no reliable single-core result for the M1 Ultra and M2 Ultra.

Cinebench 2024: cpu-monkey.com, M5 Ultra from notebookcheck.net. Cinebench 2024 and 2026 use different scales. Because Cinebench values mix two sources, chips with the same CPU (e.g. Pro and Max) can land within noise of each other or swap places.
