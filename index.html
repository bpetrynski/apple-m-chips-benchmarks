Apple M1 to M6 benchmarks: Geekbench 7 and Cinebench

Every Apple Silicon chip from M1 to M6 on one chart. Each row is a generation, each dot is a chip variant: base, Pro, Max and Ultra. Key takeaways

  • The base M6 scores 4,004 in Geekbench 7 single-core, 83% more than the M1 (2,191) and higher than any M5 chip (best: M5 Max, 3,733). In Cinebench 2026 single-core it's 824 vs 419 for the M1, almost exactly 2×.
  • In multi-core the base M6 (21,868 in Geekbench 7) is 2.6× the M1 and beats the M2 Max and M3 Pro, but not the M4 Pro.
  • The fastest chip is the M5 Ultra: 51,848 in Geekbench 7 multi-core, 343,652 in Metal and 16,971 in Cinebench 2026 multi-core. That's 32% (CPU) and 39% (GPU) more than the M3 Ultra.
  • Within one generation single-core barely changes, because base, Pro, Max and Ultra use the same CPU cores. The differences show up in multi-core and GPU.
  • There is no M4 Ultra. Apple skipped it, so the Ultra line goes from M3 Ultra to M5 Ultra.

Sources and method

Geekbench 7: Geekbench Browser, Mac Benchmarks, average of submitted results. For each chip the best-scoring config and Mac model is used, e.g. the M5 Max is the 18 CPU / 40 GPU version. Geekbench 7 uses a different scale than Geekbench 6, so don't compare these numbers with older GB6 charts.

Cinebench 2026: cpu-monkey.com. Multi-core for M2 Max, M2 Ultra and M3 Pro and single-core for M2 Max, M3 Pro, M5 Pro and M5 Max come from notebookcheck.net. The M1 Ultra has no measured multi-core result; its value is an estimate from Drop & Render (hollow dot). There's no reliable single-core result for the M1 Ultra and M2 Ultra.

Cinebench 2024: cpu-monkey.com, M5 Ultra from notebookcheck.net. Cinebench 2024 and 2026 use different scales.
